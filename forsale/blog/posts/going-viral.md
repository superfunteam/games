A daily game does not go viral because you added a share button. It goes viral because the player finishes with something they want to say. Your job is to hand them that feeling, and then get out of the way.

Here is our short list.

## 1. Fun gameplay

Obviously. But worth stating, because no amount of clever sharing mechanics rescues a game people do not enjoy playing. If the core loop is not good, everything below is decoration.

## 2. Players must feel clever

This is the real engine. Nobody shares a puzzle they merely completed. They share a puzzle they *cracked*.

That means the solution has to arrive as a small revelation: the moment where the trick clicks and the player feels a half-step smarter than they did a minute ago. Design for that click.

::: quote
Nobody posts a screenshot to say "I did the thing." They post it to say "look what I figured out."
:::

::: game flipwords
:::

## 3. Or they must have JUST missed

The flip side, and just as powerful. A near miss is intensely shareable, because it comes with a built-in argument.

One stroke over par. One letter away. A guess that was defensible and still wrong. Those players open the group chat to plead their case, and pleading your case is sharing.

The trick is that a near miss has to feel *fair*. If the player thinks the game cheated, they do not post it, they just leave.

::: game links
:::

## 4. A decent share bug

Once the feeling exists, it needs a container. The share artifact should be readable in a text message with no context, spoiler-free, and instantly recognizable as your game.

Emoji grids caught on for a reason: they show the shape of your attempt without giving away the answer. Copy the principle, not the squares.

## 5. Or a friend group

If you can skip public sharing entirely, do. The strongest daily habits are small and social: a group of four coworkers, a family thread, two friends who have been trading scores for a year.

Codes, private leaderboards, and head-to-head links all work here. Public virality is a spike. A friend group is a retention plan.

:::

Put simply: make the player feel something worth reporting, then make it easy to report. Do the first part and the second is nearly free. Skip the first part and no share button will save you.
